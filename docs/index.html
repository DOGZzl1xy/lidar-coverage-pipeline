396 county subdivisions in the contiguous U.S. lack modern LiDAR; which list to use

I measured how much of each of the 35,309 county subdivisions in the 48 contiguous states and DC is covered by LiDAR collected in 2015 or later. When every collection USGS has published is counted, 396 have less than 5% coverage. The hobuinc inventory named in the original brief gives 2,304, but most of that difference is published 3DEP data the inventory has not added yet. I recommend treating the 396 list as primary and keeping the 2,304 list as a secondary view.

Most of the 2,304 gaps are 3DEP data the inventory has not added yet

The hobuinc inventory lists only collections that have already been converted to Entwine Point Tiles. I checked several towns that it showed with 0% coverage against the official USGS 3DEP index. Concord, NC has NC_Phase4_Cabarrus_2016, New Ulm, MN has MN_RiverEast_2_B23, and Colonial Heights, VA has VA_SouthamptonHenricoWMBG_2_2019. None of these is in the inventory. Adding the official index removes 1,908 of the 2,304 gaps. Minnesota drops from 548 to 14, and Pennsylvania (123) and New Jersey (142) drop to zero.

Meredith township, NC remains a gap in both sources, because the only data covering it is from 2001.

The remaining gaps are concentrated in five states

North Carolina alone accounts for 157 of the 396 (40%). Together with Oregon (46), Mississippi (37), Virginia (28) and North Dakota (19), five states hold 72% of the list. Twenty-nine of the 49 jurisdictions have no gaps.

I checked the dates of 53 disputed collections one by one

Collection years come from their names unless a review found otherwise. I reviewed every collection whose name gave no year, and every one whose name and official USGS dates put it on different sides of 2015, against USGS, OpenTopography, NOAA and state records. For 36 of them I also sampled GPS times from the point clouds (5 to 65 MB each); where a collection had published flight dates, the samples matched them to the day. Each of the 53 decisions is stored with its evidence in the code.

Two rules apply throughout. A collection whose flights span 2015 counts as modern. The "County subdivisions not defined" placeholders in the Census files, which are mostly water, are excluded.

The hobuinc list still matters if the next step needs streamable point clouds

The main argument for the 2,304 list is access. Collections in the hobuinc inventory can be streamed from usgs.entwine.io. The extra collections in the official index are only available as LAZ downloads from USGS. If later work depends on streaming point clouds, the 2,304 list shows where that is not possible today. If the question is only where modern LiDAR exists, the 396 list is the accurate one.

The 396 list has two limits. Outside the 53 reviewed collections it takes official dates at face value. Its footprints are also simplified to about 50 m, so towns near a collection edge may shift slightly.

Two states may have fewer gaps than either list shows

Minnesota's Gen2 LiDAR (2021 to 2024) and Kentucky's KyFromAbove Phases 2 and 3 (2024 to 2026) are state programs that neither source fully includes. The Minnesota and Kentucky output files carry a note to this effect. The hobuinc inventory also changes almost daily, so a rerun on another day can differ by a few towns.

Appendix: method and reproduction
  • Units: Census TIGER/Line 2024 county subdivisions. Areas computed in EPSG:5070, with overlapping footprints merged so no area counts twice.
  • Gap: less than 5% of the area covered by LiDAR whose latest acquisition year is 2015 or later.
  • Inputs are footprints and metadata. The analysis itself downloads no point clouds.
  • Each run records its parameters and the SHA-256 of every input, and a validator checks all outputs.
