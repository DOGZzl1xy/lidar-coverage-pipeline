420 county subdivisions in the contiguous U.S. lack modern LiDAR; which list to use

I measured how much of each of the 35,309 county subdivisions in the 48 contiguous states and DC is covered by LiDAR collected in 2015 or later. When every collection USGS has published is counted, 420 have less than 5% coverage. The hobuinc inventory named in the original brief gives 2,306, but most of that difference is published 3DEP data the inventory has not added yet. I recommend treating the 420 list as primary and keeping the 2,306 list as a secondary view.

The first version of this memo gave 396. Since then I checked the acquisition date of every collection against the GPS times in its point cloud. Corrected dates add 22 gaps, almost all in Allegan and Van Buren counties, Michigan; a newer inventory adds 2.

Decision requested

Most of the 2,306 gaps are 3DEP data the inventory has not added yet

The hobuinc inventory lists only collections that have already been converted to Entwine Point Tiles. I checked several towns that it showed with 0% coverage against the official USGS 3DEP index. Concord, NC has NC_Phase4_Cabarrus_2016, New Ulm, MN has MN_RiverEast_2_B23, and Colonial Heights, VA has VA_SouthamptonHenricoWMBG_2_2019. None of these is in the inventory. Adding the official index removes 1,886 of the 2,306 gaps. Minnesota drops from 549 to 14, and Pennsylvania (123) and New Jersey (142) drop to zero.

Meredith township, NC remains a gap in both sources, because the only data covering it is from 2001.

The remaining gaps are concentrated in five states

North Carolina alone accounts for 157 of the 420 (37%). Together with Oregon (45), Michigan (37), Mississippi (37) and Virginia (29), five states hold 73% of the list. Twenty-nine of the 49 jurisdictions have no gaps.

I checked every collection's date against its point cloud

I read GPS times from a sample of points in all 2,866 collections: 2,274 in the hobuinc inventory and 592 found only in the official index. 2,286 yielded dates. Of those that also have official dates, 98.4% fall in the same years. The 37 that do not mostly point to errors in the official index: 9 list a single day that looks like a publication date, 9 have the right month and day but the wrong year, and 8 Illinois District 7 counties share one range that is 1 to 2 years late. The other 578, mostly flown 2006 to 2012, store no usable timestamps.

For only 10 collections did the samples put the date on the other side of 2015 from the year the analysis used. I reviewed each and changed 8; the other 2 had too few 2015 points or too few tiles to overrule the official dates. All 10 now carry reviewed dates in the code (62 decisions in total, each with its evidence). Two of the changes affect the 420 list:

  • MI_25County_AlleganCo_2015 was flown on December 7, 2014, not in 2015. This adds 27 gaps in Allegan and Van Buren counties.
  • MI_LeelanauCo_2015 was flown in April 2015, not December 2014 as an earlier review found. This removes 5 gaps.

The others, in northern New York and the Missouri bootheel, change only the hobuinc list. Of the 48 earlier decisions the samples could test, all but the Leelanau one held.

Two rules apply throughout. A collection counts as modern if at least 5% of its sampled points, or its reviewed flight dates, fall in 2015 or later. The "County subdivisions not defined" placeholders in the Census files, which are mostly water, are excluded.

The hobuinc list still matters if the next step needs streamable point clouds

The main argument for the 2,306 list is access. Collections in the hobuinc inventory can be streamed from usgs.entwine.io. The extra collections in the official index are only available as LAZ downloads from USGS. If later work depends on streaming point clouds, the 2,306 list shows where that is not possible today. If the question is only where modern LiDAR exists, the 420 list is the accurate one.

The 420 list has two limits. For the 578 collections without usable timestamps it takes official dates at face value, and the 592 official-index collections were sampled at only 4 tiles each. Its footprints are also simplified to about 50 m, so towns near a collection edge may shift slightly.

Two states may have fewer gaps than either list shows

Minnesota's Gen2 LiDAR (2021 to 2024) and Kentucky's KyFromAbove Phases 2 and 3 (2024 to 2026) are state programs that neither source fully includes. The Minnesota and Kentucky output files carry a note to this effect. The hobuinc inventory also changes almost daily, so a rerun on another day can differ by a few towns.

Appendix: method and reproduction
  • Units: Census TIGER/Line 2024 county subdivisions. Areas computed in EPSG:5070, with overlapping footprints merged so no area counts twice.
  • Gap: less than 5% of the area covered by LiDAR whose latest acquisition year is 2015 or later.
  • Collection year: the reviewed date where one exists, otherwise the official index or the year in the collection name.
  • Inputs are footprints and metadata. The analysis itself downloads no point clouds; the date check read about 1 MB per inventory node and the first 100,000 points of each sampled LAZ tile.
  • Each run records its parameters and the SHA-256 of every input, and a validator checks all outputs.
